Notebook C — Sudan DRE settlement atlas¶

Purpose. Inspect the World Bank / VIDA Distributed Renewable Energy Atlas settlement release (June 2025), check what can be reproduced, and prepare a spatial input for a future grid / mini-grid / standalone comparison.

Reading order: study plan → evidence and gaps → this notebook. Notebook A is a 2022 regional dispatch model; Notebook B investigates a 2012 planning report. This notebook does not run a post-war least-cost electrification optimization.

The source is the Sudan DRE download, the accompanying data dictionary and the VIDA DRE Atlas Planning Platform — Technical Documentation supplied for this project. See source register and data lineage.

1. Definitions before numbers¶

Field What it means What it cannot establish
num_connections Buildings ≥15 m² treated as potential connections Metered customers, households or actual access
demand Daily kWh calculated from building-size counts at fixed low service values Measured load, industrial/critical demand, hourly peaks or a 2022 observation
distance_to_existing_transmission_lines Straight-line km to source grid geography of mixed/unknown voltage A feasible connection route, spare capacity or a functioning post-war line
has_nightlight VIIRS light overlap, September 2022–August 2023 Household electricity access; observation period spans before and after April 2023
security_risk ACLED fatalities near the settlement in 2020–2022 Current security, conflict damage or safety for travel/construction
pv_value Long-run annual optimum PV yield, 1994–2015, kWh/kWp Hourly solar generation or usable siting area

The dataset release year is 2025. The individual input layers have different dates. The atlas is a spatial screening source, not a uniform 2022 or current-year snapshot. Low historical exposure must never be interpreted as safe today.

The 2022 comparison uses the national consumer electricity figure already documented in Notebook A. It shows why the atlas demand cannot simply replace national load: it implies roughly 2.29 TWh/year at the prescribed low service level versus 13.261 TWh of national consumer electricity in 2022. Commercial, industrial, public-service and suppressed demand, actual connection status and service quality need separate treatment. No nationwide scaling factor is inferred from this mismatch.

7. What this enables, and what remains¶

Now possible: choose representative settlement clusters across location, size, grid-distance and road-access conditions; construct demand scenarios with traceable origins; compare the published risk labels to their underlying 2020–2022 fatality rule; compare modeled regional distributions; and review the original polygons in GIS. The saved source is released under CC BY 4.0 with attribution in SOURCE_REGISTER.md.

Still required for least-cost choices: verified usable/repairable grid geometry and capacity; realistic candidate routes and transformers; damage/repair scenarios; household, public-service and productive demand with hourly profiles and comparable service standards; 2022/post-war calibration data; PV/hydro/wind chronology; lifecycle technology/distribution/connection costs; finance assumptions; and independent local validation. A 2009 transmission shapefile exists but predates South Sudan's separation and the war and cannot establish functioning current lines. See the study plan.

Next experiment: select a small, explicitly documented set of diverse clusters, verify their network context, specify the same service requirement for grid, mini-grid and standalone systems, and run a cost-complete sensitivity analysis. Expand nationally only after the pilot checks pass.
