Sudan settlement recovery: OnSSET + PyPSA¶

Research question: Where, and under what recovery conditions, are grid rehabilitation or extension, mini-grids or standalone systems the least-cost way to provide reliable electricity in Sudan?

This is the active entry point. Native OnSSET technology costs screen 11,268 settlements in six states. Three completed PyPSA cases supply recovery assumptions and national/corridor context. The models are linked one way; results are conditional rankings, not verified post-war access or project recommendations.

See method and reproduction, guide to previous work and the full-year PyPSA study.

1. What the data actually describe¶

The DRE atlas gives modeled population, building-based potential connections, synthetic low-service demand and distance to a mapped existing line. It does not measure access, sales or war damage. This run targets at least 1 kWh/day per potential connection. A line within 50 km is only a grid candidate: voltage, condition, route and spare capacity are unknown. OnSSET's PV/battery packages are simplified cost equivalents without hourly storage or diesel adequacy checks. See the full assumptions.

2. Recovery scenarios and PyPSA supply context¶

Restricted: no repair, six assumed local outage hours/day. Selective: repairs within a $45m budget, three assumed outage hours/day. **Restored:** all four candidate corridors repaired, no scheduled local outage. These are analyst scenarios, not measured outages. The OnSSET grid ranking changes through an explicit $0.45/kWh reliable-backup proxy for outage energy, relative to the $0.15/kWh planning bulk price. Repair spending is shown separately. PyPSA system cost includes national operation and overlaps with the new screen's service costs; never add the two totals.

6. How robust are ranking changes?¶

A small gap between the cheapest and second-cheapest option means the choice may move with better input data. The old atlas security label is not used as a current damage-cost multiplier.

7. Validation and the next research step¶

The code checks settlement coverage, scenario rows, finite selected costs and saved PyPSA case IDs. This establishes computational reproducibility, not empirical validation. For publishable recommendations, verify baseline access, demand and displacement; feeder/substation voltage and spare capacity; surveyed damage and repair costs; and a reliable hourly service target. Calibrate OnSSET cost curves with representative hourly off-grid designs. Then iterate: aggregate candidate grid load to PyPSA buses, optimize supply/repair/dispatch, return bus-specific cost/capacity/reliability to OnSSET, and repeat. See the workspace guide.
